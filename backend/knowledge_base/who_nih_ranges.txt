# WHO and NIH General Reference Guidelines

This guide consolidates recommendations from the World Health Organization (WHO) and the National Institutes of Health (NIH) regarding global health markers, diagnostic criteria, and standard screening intervals.

## Core Metabolic Guidelines
- **Fasting Plasma Glucose**: WHO recommends screening for diabetes starting at age 45 or earlier in high-risk individuals. Standard thresholds identify diabetes at fasting levels >= 126 mg/dL.
- **Cardiovascular Risk Screening**: Total cholesterol should ideally remain under 200 mg/dL. High LDL-C (>190 mg/dL) warrants pharmacotherapy (such as statins) regardless of general risk factors.
- **Anemia Thresholds**: WHO defines anemia in males as Hb < 13.0 g/dL, and in non-pregnant females as Hb < 12.0 g/dL.

## Essential Screening Frequency
- **Lipid Panel**: Every 4 to 6 years for healthy adults starting at age 20. More frequent screening is recommended for diabetes, hypertension, or a family history of premature heart disease.
- **Blood Glucose**: Every 3 years starting at age 45, or annually if overweight with additional risk factors (e.g., prediabetes, gestational diabetes history).
- **Renal & Hepatic Profiles**: Part of the annual physical checkup for patients over 50, or for those managing diabetes, hypertension, or taking hepatotoxic or nephrotoxic medications.

## Diagnostic Standards for Cardiovascular and Renal Health
- **Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)**: Persistently decreased eGFR (<60 mL/min/1.73m²) or presence of albuminuria (>30 mg/g creatinine) for more than 3 months indicates CKD.
- **Hypertension**: A single high BP reading does not confirm hypertension. Diagnoses require multiple elevated readings across separate clinical encounters.
- **Hypo- and Hyper-thyroidism**: Diagnosed by assessing serum TSH first. If abnormal, Free T4 testing is conducted to determine clinical vs subclinical status.
