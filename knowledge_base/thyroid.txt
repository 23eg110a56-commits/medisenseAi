# Thyroid Profile Reference Guide

A Thyroid Profile evaluates the function of the thyroid gland, which controls the body's metabolism and energy usage.

## Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH)
- **Standard Range**: 0.4 to 4.5 mIU/L
- **High TSH**: Indicates Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid). The pituitary gland produces extra TSH to stimulate the thyroid.
- **Low TSH**: Indicates Hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid) or pituitary insufficiency. The thyroid is overproducing hormones, causing the pituitary to scale back TSH.

## Free Thyroxine (Free T4)
- **Standard Range**: 0.8 to 1.8 ng/dL
- **Clinical Significance**: Free T4 is the active form of thyroxine. Elevated Free T4 indicates hyperthyroidism. Low Free T4 indicates hypothyroidism.

## Free Triiodothyronine (Free T3)
- **Standard Range**: 2.0 to 4.4 pg/mL
- **Clinical Significance**: Free T3 is often measured to diagnose hyperthyroidism or monitor its treatment. High levels occur in hyperthyroidism, while low levels can occur in severe non-thyroidal illness.

## Hypothyroidism (Underactive Thyroid)
- **Lab Profile**: High TSH, low or low-normal Free T4/Free T3.
- **Symptoms**: Fatigue, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, thinning hair, cold intolerance, depression, slow heart rate.
- **Specialist**: Endocrinologist.

## Hyperthyroidism (Overactive Thyroid)
- **Lab Profile**: Low TSH, high or high-normal Free T4/Free T3.
- **Symptoms**: Weight loss, rapid or irregular heartbeat (tachycardia), anxiety, irritability, heat intolerance, sweating, hand tremors, frequent bowel movements.
- **Specialist**: Endocrinologist.
